# Lemon Battery
e=🍋 a=8-13 d=Medium t=40 min
m: 4 lemons; 4 galvanized (zinc-coated) nails; 4 copper coins or copper strips; alligator clip wires; small red LED
- Roll each lemon on the table to loosen the juice inside.
- Push a nail and a copper coin into each lemon, about 2 cm apart.
- Connect the copper of one lemon to the nail of the next with a wire.
- Connect the free copper and free nail to the two LED legs.
- If the LED does not light, flip it around.
w: Lemon juice is an acid that lets electrons flow between two different metals. Each lemon makes a little less than 1 volt. Linking them adds the voltages together.
x: Try potatoes, oranges and pickles. Which fruit makes the most power?
s: Do not eat the lemons afterwards.

# Homemade Electromagnet
e=🧲 a=9-13 d=Medium t=30 min
m: large iron nail; 1 m of thin insulated copper wire; D battery; electrical tape; paper clips

- Strip the plastic off both wire ends (adult help).
- Tape the wire ends to the two ends of the battery.

# Conductor or Insulator?
e=🔌 a=6-12 d=Easy t=30 min
m: battery; bulb or LED; 3 wires; spoon; coin; eraser; foil; pencil lead; plastic; wood; key
- Make a circuit with a gap between two wire ends.
- Touch both ends to an object.
- If the bulb lights, it is a conductor. If not, it is an insulator.

w: Metals let electrons move freely, so they conduct electricity. Plastic, wood and rubber hold electrons tightly, so they are insulators.
