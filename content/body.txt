# Balloon Lung Model
e=🫁 a=7-12 d=Medium t=40 min
m: plastic bottle; 2 balloons; scissors; tape; rubber band; straw (optional)
- An adult cuts off the bottom of a plastic bottle.
- Push one balloon into the bottle neck and stretch its opening over the rim.
- Cut the top off the second balloon and stretch it over the open bottom. Tape it.
- Pull the bottom balloon down and watch the inside balloon fill.
- Push it up and watch it empty.
w: The bottom balloon works like your diaphragm muscle. When it moves down, it makes room for air to rush into your lungs.
x: Add a Y-straw and two balloons to model both lungs.

# Heart Rate and Exercise
e=💓 a=7-13 d=Easy t=30 min
m: stopwatch; notebook; space to move
- Find your pulse on your wrist or neck.
- Count beats for 15 seconds and multiply by 4.
- Do jumping jacks for 1 minute.
- Measure again right away.
- Measure every minute until it returns to normal.
w: Your muscles need more oxygen when you exercise, so your heart beats faster to pump more blood.
x: Compare walking, running and dancing.

e=🕵️ a=6-12 d=Easy t=30 min
m: pencil; paper; clear tape; magnifier; cocoa powder; soft brush; glass

# Reaction Time Ruler Drop
e=📏 a=7-13 d=Easy t=20 min
m: 30 cm ruler; friend; notebook
- Have a friend hold a ruler vertically.
- Place your fingers at the 0 mark without touching.
- When they drop it, catch it as fast as you can.
- Record the number where you caught it.
- Try 5 times and average.
w: Your eyes send a message to your brain, which tells your fingers to move. The shorter the distance, the faster your reaction.
x: Test your other hand, or test after playing a video game.

# Find Your Blind Spot

m: index card; marker

# Pupil Light Response
e=👀 a=6-11 d=Easy t=10 min
m: hand mirror; desk lamp; dim room; friend
- Sit in a dim room for 2 minutes with a friend.
- Look closely at your friend's pupils, the black circles in the eyes.
- Turn on a lamp beside them (not shining into the eyes).
- Watch the pupils shrink within a second.
- Turn it off and watch them grow again.
w: The iris is a muscle that changes the pupil's size. In dim light it opens wide to let in more light, and in bright light it closes to protect the eye.
x: Close one eye for a minute, then open it. Did both pupils change?
s: Never shine bright lights directly into eyes.
