# Cloud in a Jar
e=☁️ a=6-12 d=Easy t=15 min
m: glass jar with lid; hot water; ice cubes; hairspray or a lit match blown out (adult); flashlight
- An adult pours 2-3 cm of hot water into the jar and swirls it.
- Turn the lid upside down, fill it with ice and set it on the jar.
- After 30 seconds, lift the lid and an adult adds a quick spray of hairspray.
- Replace the lid and watch a cloud form.
- Lift the lid and watch the cloud escape.
w: Warm moist air rises and cools under the ice. Water vapor condenses on tiny particles from the hairspray, forming a cloud of droplets.
x: Shine a flashlight through the jar to see the cloud swirl.
s: Adults handle hot water and spray.

# Wind Vane
e=🧭 a=6-11 d=Easy t=30 min
m: pencil with eraser; drinking straw; straight pin; card stock; paper cup; modeling clay; marker; compass app
- Cut a triangle arrow tip and a larger square tail from card stock.
- Cut small slits in each end of the straw and slide in the tip and tail.
- Push the pin through the middle of the straw into the pencil eraser so it spins freely.
- Stand the pencil in a clay-filled cup and mark N, E, S and W on the cup.
- Take it outside, line N up with north using a compass, and watch where the arrow points.
w: The bigger tail catches more wind, so it swings away and the arrow points into the wind. Wind is named for where it comes from, so a north wind blows from the north.
x: Record the wind direction and the weather for a week. Is there a pattern?

# Bottle Thermometer
e=🌡️ a=8-12 d=Medium t=30 min
m: small clear bottle; water; rubbing alcohol (adult); clear straw; modeling clay; food coloring; bowls of hot and cold water
- Fill the bottle one quarter with equal parts water and rubbing alcohol.
- Add a few drops of food coloring.
- Put the straw in without touching the bottom and seal around it with clay.
- Hold the bottle with both hands and watch the liquid climb.
- Place it in hot water, then cold water, and mark the levels.
w: Liquids expand when warmed and contract when cooled. The narrow straw makes the small change easy to see, just like a real thermometer.
x: Calibrate it by marking levels next to a real thermometer.
s: Adults handle rubbing alcohol. Do not drink.

# Thunderstorm Distance Counting
e=⛈️ a=7-12 d=Easy t=15 min
m: stopwatch or counting; notebook; a safe indoor window during a storm
- When you see a lightning flash, start counting seconds.
- Stop when you hear the thunder.
- Divide the seconds by 3 for kilometers or by 5 for miles.
- Repeat for several flashes and write the distances.
- Decide if the storm is coming closer or moving away.
w: Light reaches you almost instantly, but sound travels about 1 km in 3 seconds. The gap tells you how far away the lightning was.
x: Make a graph of distance vs time during one storm.
s: Stay indoors during thunderstorms. If you can hear thunder, you are close enough to be struck.
