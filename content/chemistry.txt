# Classic Baking Soda Volcano
e=🌋 a=4-10 d=Easy t=20 min
m: small plastic bottle; modeling clay or play dough; tray; 2 tbsp baking soda; 1/2 cup vinegar; dish soap; red food coloring
- Stand the bottle on a tray and build a clay mountain around it, leaving the opening clear.
- Add the baking soda, a squirt of dish soap and a few drops of red food coloring into the bottle.
- Pour the vinegar in quickly and step back.
- Watch the foamy lava bubble out and flow down the sides.
- Wipe out the bottle and try again with different amounts.
w: Vinegar (an acid) reacts with baking soda (a base) to make carbon dioxide gas. The gas gets trapped in the soapy liquid, making foam that rushes out of the bottle.
x: Measure how far the lava flows using 1, 2 and 3 spoons of baking soda.

# Lava Lamp in a Bottle
e=🫧 a=5-11 d=Easy t=20 min
m: clear plastic bottle; water; vegetable oil; food coloring; fizzy antacid tablet
- Fill the bottle about one quarter full with water.
- Fill almost to the top with vegetable oil and wait for the layers to settle.
- Add 8 to 10 drops of food coloring and watch the drops sink through the oil.
- Break a fizzy tablet into pieces and drop one piece in.
- Watch colored blobs rise and fall. Add another piece when it stops.
w: Oil is lighter (less dense) than water, and the two do not mix. The tablet releases gas bubbles that carry colored water up; at the top the gas escapes and the heavier water sinks again.
x: Shine a flashlight under the bottle in a dark room for a glowing lamp.
s: Adults should handle the tablets. Do not drink the mixture.

# Kid-Safe Elephant Toothpaste
e=🐘 a=6-12 d=Medium t=25 min
m: clean plastic bottle; 1/2 cup 3% hydrogen peroxide; 1 tbsp dry yeast; 3 tbsp warm water; dish soap; food coloring; tray; safety goggles
- Put on goggles and stand the bottle in a big tray.
- Pour the hydrogen peroxide into the bottle with a big squirt of dish soap and some food coloring.
- In a cup, mix the yeast with warm water for 30 seconds.
- Pour the yeast mixture into the bottle and step back.
- Touch the outside of the bottle after the foam erupts. It feels warm!
w: Yeast contains an enzyme called catalase that breaks hydrogen peroxide into water and oxygen very quickly. The oxygen gets trapped by the soap, making a huge column of foam. The bottle warms because the reaction gives off heat.
x: Compare tall thin bottles with short wide ones to see which makes the tallest foam.
s: Wear goggles. Use only 3% drugstore hydrogen peroxide and have an adult pour it.

# Oobleck: Solid or Liquid?
e=🥣 a=3-10 d=Easy t=15 min
m: 1 cup cornstarch; 1/2 cup water; bowl; food coloring (optional); tray
- Pour the cornstarch into a bowl.
- Slowly stir in water a little at a time until the mixture is thick.
- Punch the surface quickly. It feels hard!
- Now slowly dip your fingers in. They sink like it is a liquid.
- Roll some into a ball, then stop rolling and watch it melt.
w: Oobleck is a non-Newtonian fluid. When you push hard, the cornstarch grains jam together and act like a solid. When you move slowly, they slide past each other and flow like a liquid.
x: Put oobleck on a speaker cone wrapped in plastic and play bass music to see it dance.
s: Throw oobleck in the trash, not down the sink. It can clog drains.

# Growing Salt Crystals

- Ask an adult for a cup of very hot tap water in a jar.

# Rock Candy on a Stick

- An adult heats the water and stirs in sugar until it all dissolves, then lets it cool for 20 minutes.

# The Naked Egg
e=🥚 a=6-12 d=Easy t=2-3 days
