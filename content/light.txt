# Rainbow in a Glass of Water
e=🌈 a=4-10 d=Easy t=15 min
m: clear glass of water; sunny window or flashlight; white paper
- Fill a glass to the top with water.
- Put it on a sunny windowsill.
- Hold white paper on the floor and move it until a rainbow appears.
- Try with a flashlight in a dark room.
w: White light is made of all colors. When light bends through water, each color bends a different amount, so they spread out into a rainbow.
x: Use a mirror in a tray of water to make an even brighter rainbow.

# CD Spectroscope
e=💿 a=8-13 d=Medium t=30 min
m: old CD; cereal box; scissors; tape; foil
- Cut a thin slit in the top of the box at one end.
- Cut a viewing hole at the other end, angled down.
- Tape a piece of CD inside at an angle under the viewing hole.
- Point the slit at a light and look through the hole.
- Compare sunlight, LED lights and old bulbs.
w: The tiny grooves on a CD split light into its colors. Different lights have different color patterns, called spectra.
x: Look at a candle flame, a neon sign and a phone screen.
s: Never look directly at the sun.

# Pinhole Camera
e=📷 a=8-13 d=Medium t=40 min
m: shoebox or tube; foil; pin; wax paper; tape; black paper; scissors
- Cut a small square in one end of the box and tape foil over it.
- Poke a tiny pinhole in the foil.
- Cut out the other end and tape wax paper over it as a screen.
- Wrap black paper around to block extra light.
- Point the pinhole at a bright scene and look at the wax paper.
w: Light travels in straight lines. Rays from the top of an object cross through the pinhole and hit the bottom of the screen, so the image appears upside down.
x: Make the hole bigger. What happens to the brightness and sharpness?

# Disappearing Coin
e=🪙 a=6-11 d=Easy t=10 min
m: coin; opaque cup; water
- Put a coin in the bottom of the cup.
- Move back until the edge just hides the coin.
- Keep your head still while a friend slowly pours water in.

# Light Follows Water
e=💦 a=9-13 d=Medium t=20 min
m: clear plastic bottle; nail (adult); bright flashlight or laser pointer (adult only); tray; dark room
- An adult pokes a hole near the bottom of the bottle.
- Cover the hole and fill the bottle with water.
- Shine the flashlight through the bottle from the other side, aimed at the hole.
- Uncover the hole and watch the stream glow.
w: Light bounces along inside the stream instead of escaping, called total internal reflection. Fiber optic cables carry internet signals this way.
x: Add a drop of milk to the water to see the light path better.
s: Never point lasers at eyes.

# Mixing Colors of Light
e=🔴 a=7-12 d=Easy t=20 min
m: 3 flashlights; red, green and blue cellophane; rubber bands; white wall
- Cover each flashlight with a different color.
- Shine red and green onto the wall where they overlap.
- Try green and blue, then red and blue.
- Shine all three together.
w: Colored light mixes differently from paint. Red and green light make yellow, and all three make white. This is how screens make colors.
x: Look at a screen with a magnifier and see the tiny red, green and blue dots.

# Why Is the Sky Blue?
e=🌅 a=7-12 d=Easy t=15 min
m: clear glass of water; milk; flashlight; dark room
- Fill the glass with water and add a few drops of milk.
- Shine a flashlight through the side.
- Look at the glass from the side. It looks bluish.
- Look through the glass at the flashlight. It looks orange.
w: Tiny particles scatter blue light more than red light. In the sky, air scatters blue light everywhere. At sunset, light travels through more air, so mostly reds and oranges reach us.
x: Add more milk and see the sunset color get deeper.

# Afterimage Illusion
e=👁️ a=6-12 d=Easy t=10 min
m: drawing with bright colors (a red heart or a flag); white paper; timer
- Stare at a dot in the middle of the colored picture for 30 seconds without moving your eyes.
- Quickly look at a white paper.
- Watch the picture appear in opposite colors.
- Try with different colors.
w: Color sensors in your eyes get tired after staring at one color. When you look at white, the tired sensors respond less, so you see the opposite color.
x: Draw a green, black and yellow flag that shows a red, white and blue afterimage.

# Speed of Light with Chocolate
e=🍫 a=11-14 d=Medium t=20 min
m: chocolate bar; microwave with the turntable removed; plate; ruler; calculator
- An adult removes the turntable and puts the chocolate on a plate.
- Microwave on low for 15 to 20 seconds until spots melt.
- Measure the distance between melted spots.
- Double it, then multiply by the frequency on the microwave label (usually 2,450,000,000).
w: Microwaves make standing waves with hot spots half a wavelength apart. Wavelength times frequency equals the speed of light, about 300,000 km per second.
x: How close did you get to the real speed of light?
s: Adult supervision. Do not run the microwave empty or too long.
