# Oreo Moon Phases
e=🌙 a=5-10 d=Easy t=30 min
m: 8 sandwich cookies; plastic knife; paper plate; moon phase chart
- Twist apart each cookie so the cream stays on one side.
- Scrape away cream to match each phase: new, crescent, quarter, gibbous, full.

# Pocket Solar System
e=📏 a=8-13 d=Easy t=20 min
m: strip of paper about 1 m long; markers; ruler
- Write the Sun at one end and Neptune at the other.
- Fold in half: mark Uranus at the fold.
- Fold the Sun end to Uranus: mark Saturn. Fold the Sun to Saturn: mark Jupiter.
- Fold the Sun to Jupiter: mark the asteroid belt. Then fold again and place Mars, Earth, Venus and Mercury close to the Sun.
- Look at how crowded the inner planets are.
w: The inner planets are bunched close to the Sun, while the giants are spread far apart. Models like this show distances in a way numbers can't.
x: Add the dwarf planet Pluto beyond Neptune.

# Pinhole Sun Measurement
e=☀️ a=10-14 d=Medium t=30 min
m: card with a clean pinhole; white paper on a clipboard; meter stick; calculator
- Stand with your back to the Sun.
- Hold the pinhole card so it projects a small circle (the Sun's image) onto the paper 1 m behind it.
- Measure the diameter of the image in millimeters.
- Divide image width by the distance from pinhole to paper.
- Multiply by 150,000,000 km to estimate the Sun's diameter.
w: The image and the Sun form similar triangles. Knowing the Sun's distance lets you calculate its size, about 1.4 million km across.
x: How close did your answer get? What caused the error?
s: Never look at the Sun directly.
