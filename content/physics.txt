# Balloon Rocket on a String
e=🎈 a=5-11 d=Easy t=20 min
m: long balloon; string (3-5 m); drinking straw; tape; two chairs
- Thread the string through the straw and tie each end to a chair, pulling it tight.
- Blow up the balloon and pinch the end. Do not tie it.
- Tape the balloon to the straw.
- Pull the balloon to one end, then let go.
- Measure how far it travels.
w: Air rushing out the back pushes the balloon forward. This is Newton's third law: every action has an equal and opposite reaction.
x: Make the string slope upward and see if the rocket can climb.

# Egg Drop Challenge
e=🥚 a=7-13 d=Medium t=1 hour
m: raw eggs; straws; tape; paper; cotton balls; plastic bags; string; recycled boxes
- Design a container that will protect an egg from a fall.
- Build it using only the materials provided.
- Place the egg inside.
- Drop it from a safe height chosen by an adult, like a step stool or a low balcony.

w: A falling egg breaks because it stops too suddenly. Padding, parachutes and crumple zones make the stop take longer, which reduces the force on the egg.
x: Drop from higher and higher. What is the record for your design?
s: Adults choose the drop spot. Clean up raw egg well.

# Homemade Pendulum Timer

- Tie a washer to a string and hang it from the pencil.

# Coin and Card Inertia Trick
e=🃏 a=5-10 d=Easy t=10 min
m: playing card or index card; coin; cup
- Place the card on top of the cup.
- Put the coin in the center of the card.
- Flick the card sideways quickly with your finger.
- Watch the coin drop into the cup.
w: Objects at rest want to stay at rest. This is called inertia. The card moves so fast that friction does not have time to pull the coin along.
x: Try stacking several coins. Does it still work?

# Seesaw Lever Balance
e=⚖️ a=5-10 d=Easy t=20 min
m: ruler; pencil; coins or blocks
- Lay a pencil under the middle of a ruler to make a seesaw.
- Put 2 coins on one end and 1 coin on the other.
- Slide the single coin farther out until it balances.

w: A lever lets a small force balance a bigger one if it is farther from the pivot. This is why a small child far out on a seesaw can balance a bigger child close in.

# Pepper Scatter

m: plate; water; ground pepper; dish soap; toothpick
- Pour water onto a plate.
- Sprinkle pepper over the surface.
- Dip a toothpick in dish soap.

# Hovering Ping Pong Ball

- Turn the hair dryer on cool and point it straight up.
- Place the ball in the stream of air and let go.

# Can Crush with Air Pressure
